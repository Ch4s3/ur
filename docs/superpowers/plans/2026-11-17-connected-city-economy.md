# Connected City Economy Implementation Plan

**Goal** — Let a player diagnose a neighborhood problem, make a targeted investment, and see who benefits, who pays, and whether development changes.

**Architecture** — Resolve operations at their natural timescale, summarize them by route, network, business and ward, and let the economic simulation consume those summaries. Use a deterministic regional model for the whole city and give animated agents and vehicles the same rules and capacity constraints. Inspectors and forecasts read the same state as the simulation.

**Scope** — Ship four playable phases in order: transit and jobs, utility bottlenecks, island freight, and council decisions. This document defines the design and acceptance criteria for all four.

**Technology** — March simulation, existing UR save format, and Strata rendering. World geometry stays at the current voxel scale. The new simulation runs on road links, utility links, parcels, and cargo flows.

## Shared economic rules

### Establish the baseline before changing outcomes

UR already has parcel accounts, employment and wages, a transit demand model, visible buses, connected utilities, some food shipments and stock, and monthly city finances. Preserve these entry points while replacing the specific approximations needed in each phase. In particular, replace the current household earnings bonus for a short commute with income from attainable employment. A faster trip should not itself mint wages. Likewise, replace generic import surcharges for goods covered by the freight model with the charges those goods actually incur.

- [ ] Capture a reproducible month-by-month baseline in a starter town, a congested corridor, and an island region. Record cash, budget categories, employment, occupied homes, prices, tax receipts and the rate at which zoned parcels start construction. Keep separate records for recurring operations and capital investment.
- [ ] Name the unit and accounting period of each new quantity. Route time is in simulated minutes, fares are per boarding, cargo is in units per shipment, and income and upkeep shown in projections are per month. Keep fractional monetary accruals until settlement so a small fare, duty or wage cannot disappear through repeated integer rounding.
- [ ] Compare changes from the same saved city, calendar, and seed. Show a baseline and an observed or projected outcome with the period and any concurrent changes. Player forecasts use ranges when demand or traffic can change materially.

### Track cash, stock and welfare separately

A capital purchase reduces cash when paid. Upkeep, operating costs and receipts are attributed to their service period, with cash recognized on payment. The monthly cash reconciliation is opening cash plus receipts collected minus payments made. Reserve funds for approved future payments and reduce the reservation as each payment settles. The forecast carries the unpaid portion of each commitment.

| Event | Treatment |
| --- | --- |
| Work performed | Private payroll is a business expense and household income. Municipal payroll is a treasury expense and household income. Income tax is a transfer from earnings to the treasury. Keep private and public jobs in the same labor market. |
| Fare, utility charge, rent, or toll | A charge has a payer and a recipient. Use the same amount on both sides. Credit municipal revenue only when the city receives the payment. Rent can flow to a modeled property-owner sector. |
| Imports and exports | Imports are spending outside the region and exports are receipts from outside. The treasury sees only its purchases, sales, fees and duties. Private trade affects business accounts. Apply the same rule to existing fish and farm revenue categories. A local operator's commodity sales reach the treasury only through actual taxes, fees, or sales made by the city itself. Internal island transfers stay within the region. |
| Subsidy or earmark | A subsidy is an explicit payment. Earmarks reserve spending power. Include both when testing affordability and distinguish them from receipts. |
| Saved time, cleaner air or more reliable service | Track these in minutes, exposure, reliability or access. An optional valuation belongs in a benefit calculation. Book cash only when an underlying payment changes. |

Retain existing budget categories as projections of the new accounting entries. When replacing a legacy rule, switch the corresponding payer and recipient together. Reconcile operating activity, capital outlays, subsidies, and receipts independently. Tie taxes to their defined bases after deductions and credits. Check that closing inventory equals opening inventory plus inflows minus outflows, consumption and losses. Supply forecasts and physical loading draw on the same available units.

**Reference fixture.** A municipal bus carries 300 paid fares at $1 in a month. It costs $200 in payroll and $250 in fuel, maintenance and other operating expenses. Treasury fare receipts are $300, gross spending is $450, and the net operating cost is $150 before other taxes. Drivers receive $200 gross. A $600 depot upgrade appears as a separate cash payment in the purchase month. The fare and payroll transfers do not increase regional income. Time savings are reported separately.

### Keep feedback stable and recoverable

- [ ] Simulate movements and service within the day, latch operational measures at day end, and settle durable finances, rent changes and investment over longer windows. Apply policy and topology changes prospectively. Use deterministic logic for headless runs and save settlement inputs and progress so retries and reloads cannot post a transaction twice.
- [ ] Bound behavior changes per period. Keep prices and route demand responsive, with rolling samples and a fallback for new or rare services. Use separate thresholds and sustained conditions for opening and closure. Maintain a clear distinction between a temporary delay, a shortage, and a structural failure.
- [ ] Preserve recovery options for small cities. Test existing founding sequences and old saves. Migrate fields with explicit defaults and flag projections that are still learning their baseline. Keep basic services and viable starting demand within reach of the opening budget.
- [ ] Add replay checks for duplicate billing, negative inventory, passengers disappearing on reload, resource cuts, and proposals spending the same funds. Track simulation time and memory on the same baseline saves. Keep operational sampling independent of frame rate and visibility.

## 1. Transit → jobs → development

**Player loop.** Inspect a busy bus corridor, identify whether service is infrequent, delayed or full, then change service and watch wait times, accessible jobs, local development and operating costs. Existing levers such as frequency and street upgrades provide the first comparison.

**Files and interfaces.** Use `lib/ur/fleet.march` for scheduled runs and rider flow, `lib/ur/citizens.march` for individual trips, `lib/ur/sim.march` for access, household accounts and investment, and `lib/ur/diagnosis.march`, `lib/ur/hud.march` and `lib/ur/scenario.march` for the inspector and comparison scenario. The route summary exposes scheduled and actual departures, boardings, missed boardings, travel and wait time, cancellations, service distance and cost. Access consumes those summaries alongside road and walking times.

- [ ] **Scheduled service and finite seats.** Give lines stable IDs and schedules. Frequency creates actual departures subject to available buses and drivers. Congestion and stops consume time. Alight before boarding, board in queue order up to capacity, then record who waits for another vehicle. Aggregate passenger demand and tracked citizens share the same seat count and fare rules. Track cancelled runs separately from full buses.
- [ ] **Access, employment and prices.** Compute reachable openings from door-to-door time and cash cost, with a reliability penalty based on observed service. A job requires a reachable employer, a relevant qualification and an unfilled position. Limit matching by openings and workers. Use reachable job opportunities and the resulting employment income in household offers. Draw new households from regional migration demand and work with the existing housing vacancy and price-tier rules. Relocations between neighborhoods shift demand between them. Count a new citywide job only when an employer adds a funded position. Keep travel time in access and cash fares in expenses. When improved access raises asking rents, show both commute and affordability changes and allow existing households time to adjust.
- [ ] **Pay for actual service.** Book fixed depot and vehicle ownership costs, paid shifts, and operating costs that vary with distance or hours. Collect fares on completed boardings and assign any concession subsidy to a budget. Extra buses consume road capacity. Higher frequency can fail to improve throughput if vehicles bunch or the road remains blocked.
- [ ] **Close the loop.** Add before-and-after measures for median and slow waits, riders left behind, reachable openings by qualification, fare receipts, and net city cost. Give empty parcels a distinct explanation when poor job access is the binding constraint. For each newly eligible parcel retain the previous constraint and identify the change. Build outcomes can still lag eligibility through the existing private investment cadence.
- [ ] **Gate.** On a repeatable corridor, overloaded service leaves riders behind, some cannot reach their shifts, and an access-constrained parcel stays empty. Add a departure using a staffed bus and show that queues shrink, reachable jobs increase, and the parcel becomes eligible and eventually starts construction. Repeat with a bottleneck that prevents improvement, an unaffordable fare, and a rental increase that offsets commute savings. Confirm each failure has the correct reason and the treasury pays for service delivered.

For tuning, a ten-minute reduction in travel time increases reachable jobs only where time was the binding constraint. With 100 qualified applicants and 30 reachable vacancies, additional hires are capped at 30. A route that improves access can be worth subsidizing even when its farebox falls short.

## 2. Utility networks → local service → growth

**Player loop.** Identify an overloaded feeder or pipe, see the affected parcels, compare upgrading that link with adding local supply or reducing demand, and verify the affected parcels receive service.

**Files and interfaces.** Add a focused solver in `lib/ur/utilities_network.march`. Integrate generation, treatment, growth and billing through `lib/ur/sim.march`. Extend `lib/ur/layers.march`, `lib/ur/scene.march`, `lib/ur/build.march`, and parcel diagnosis. The solver returns delivered and unmet demand by parcel, bottleneck and utilization by link, and production used by source. The existing reachability view remains the zero-capacity or disconnected case of this model.

- [ ] **Deliver through limited links.** Use separate capacity networks for power and water and track sewage collection and treatment separately. Solve deterministic capacitated flows when networks change or load is updated. Give shared links a shared capacity across directions. Parallel routes can carry spare flow. Allocate essentials first and share comparable shortages fairly. Distinguish a disconnected parcel from an overloaded link and exhausted source capacity.
- [ ] **Model dependencies with physical limits.** Pumps, desalination, recycling and treatment consume delivered power. Bound recycled water by treated sewage and recovery yield. Account for discharged and untreated sewage and attach resulting pollution to the outlet or affected area. Resolve mutual dependencies with a bounded allocation step and use the same outcome in billing, overlays and growth. Report the binding cause if a local shortage has several dependencies.
- [ ] **Price delivery and capacity correctly.** Charge per delivered unit. Treat production, imported fuel and water, and network maintenance as separate costs. A feeder upgrade buys transfer capacity and recurring upkeep. Reserve capacity for committed construction consistently so several lots cannot claim the same spare supply. Upgrade previews show construction cost, additional upkeep, estimated coverage and remaining binding constraints.
- [ ] **Gate.** With 100 supply, demand of 70 and 20 in two neighborhoods, and a 40-capacity feeder serving the first, deliveries are 40 and 20. The first neighborhood is short 30 and 40 units of source capacity remain unused. Raising total generation alone leaves the shortfall in place. Upgrading the feeder to 70 serves both neighborhoods and raises billable delivery to 90. Exercise a damaged parallel link, a powered water plant losing power, equal-priority rationing, and a save from before capacity was tracked. A utility-blocked parcel should report the local constraint and clear only when its service can be reserved.

## 3. Island freight → prices → neighborhood demand

**Player loop.** Expand to a productive island, build a working transfer chain, reserve freight capacity, and choose how much storage and backup supply to pay for. A disrupted route leaves visible stock that runs out over time and creates new choices.

**Files and interfaces.** Extend food and freight routing in `lib/ur/food.march` and `lib/ur/fleet.march`, with inventories and cost settlement connected through `lib/ur/sim.march` and `lib/ur/save.march`. A shipment has identity, commodity, quantity, owner, origin, destination, reserved leg, and status. Stock is available only on arrival and unloading. Cargo and prices are consumed by household and business accounts.

- [ ] **Use a complete shipment chain.** Start with food, fuel and exported waste. Track stock at producers, docks, receiving markets and facilities. Cap road transport, handling, berth departures, cargo space and destination storage. Reserve space once per departure. Use common capacity for competing cargo in consistent space units. Let the player choose minimum shares or priorities for essentials, with unused space released to other cargo. Pickup and departure use physically available stock.
- [ ] **Settle purchases and prices from what arrived.** For purchased inventory, record supplier price, private delivery and handling charges, and any applicable import duty in landed cost. Use a consistent inventory-cost rule when selling. Let lost stock and constrained throughput affect costs and achievable sales. Prices respond to available stock and willingness to pay, with import alternatives setting a ceiling only when capacity and purchasing power can support them. Unsold stock, unmet demand and affordability losses stay visible. Business revenue comes from units sold. Deduct input cost once and limit demand by household disposable income or another modeled customer sector.
- [ ] **Make delays and scarcity visible.** Show stock on hand, reserved cargo, expected days of cover, pending sailings, and where a shipment is held. Link shortage explanations to facility downtime, household food costs and affected parcels. Allow buffer stocks, substitute sources, more capacity, and changed priority, each with cash costs or competing uses. Calculate island investment recovery using incremental net receipts and operating costs, with uncertainty for downtime and price changes.
- [ ] **Gate.** Send 60 food units and 40 fuel units through a departure with 100 compatible units of space. A waste shipment must wait or displace some cargo under the chosen priorities. A disconnected port cannot dispatch or deliver. A late ship leaves goods in transit while inventory is consumed. Test perishability, full storage, lost routes, reloading en route and treasury versus private cash reconciliation. Verify that a reliable competing import route limits scarcity pricing, and that shipping goods between two local docks cannot create export receipts or repeated duties.

**Cost fixture.** A private importer receives 10 units at $4 each, pays $10 in private freight and $5 in duty, and lands stock worth $55. Selling six at $7 yields $42 revenue and $33 cost of goods sold, leaving $22 in inventory and a $9 gross margin before other expenses. The city books $5 duty. In a later shipment, keep late cargo out of saleable inventory until unloaded and settle its charges once according to the purchase contract.

## 4. Neighborhood outcomes → council politics

**Player loop.** Use outcomes from the first three phases to negotiate projects and operating budgets. Councillors represent competing local interests and residents can see what a promise delivered.

**Files and interfaces.** Extend `lib/ur/council.march`, `lib/ur/diagnosis.march`, `lib/ur/hud.march` and save state. Proposals contain a priced action, funding source, expected ward effects, a completion deadline, and a baseline snapshot. Ward reports consume observed travel, outage, price, pollution and employment summaries.

- [ ] **Bring competing proposals to the same budget.** Allow multiple active proposals from different wards. Record capital cost, continuing obligations, available unrestricted funds and any restricted funding before a vote. Reserve funding for passed commitments exactly once. Reconcile proposals contingent on the same prerequisite or competing for the same capacity, and release funds on cancellation. Let the player stage, revise or reject an unaffordable project.
- [ ] **Represent impacts and coalitions.** Give ward priorities and members’ voting intentions understandable drivers. Compare who gets better job access, reliable service or lower prices, where traffic and pollution are displaced, and who bears higher rates or loses parking. Keep tax transfers separate from resource costs. Use service outcomes and distribution alongside costs when evaluating proposals. State assumptions behind uncertain forecasts.
- [ ] **Score delivery after a reasonable observation period.** Compare promised and observed metrics against the saved baseline, normalize by population served as appropriate, and show major concurrent changes such as a storm or a route closure. Give credit for sustained improvements and track harmful spillovers. Avoid counting the same investment repeatedly or rewarding a ward for short-lived oscillations. Preserve existing elections, support and relationships as the political consequence layer.
- [ ] **Gate.** Give two wards proposals that each fit alone and exceed the budget together. Approving one reserves its funds and leaves the other subject to revised scope or funding. A route improvement can help its riders and create a credible complaint in a neighboring ward. The council reacts when measured results arrive. Test conflicting commitments, cancelled projects, changed ward boundaries, election turnover and legacy saves. An accounting replay must match the player's visible approved budget.

## Verification and release order

Run the existing March simulation suite and release build for each phase, plus a focused native demo from a documented starting state. In the corridor demo, a player should be able to explain why a particular parcel started building, how much service cost, and whether any costs shifted to other households or the city. Carry that same neighborhood into the utility and freight phases. Start council integration once all three upstream outcome reports are stable enough to support promises and votes.

Use side-by-side replays to tune a healthy starter city, a congested city with room to grow, and a city recovering from a cut line or delayed sailing. Treat reconciled cash and inventory, save/reload continuity, recoverability and inspectable explanations as release gates. Starting capacities, fares, pricing sensitivities and voting thresholds are tuning values. The numerical fixtures above define invariants for the tests.
